# 01 — Positioning

## The one-sentence pitch

> *"Physicist and systems engineer who ships unusually fast — now building empirical evals and reliability tooling for agentic LLMs, aimed at frontier safety work."*

Tape this to your monitor. Every project decision, every blog paragraph, every application essay should be defensible against this sentence. If it doesn't reinforce the sentence, cut it.

## What your edge IS

| Edge | How it shows up in artifacts |
|---|---|
| Physics reasoning | Phase-diagram / scaling-law framings; clean analytical writeups; thinking in invariants |
| Systems engineering | Clean repo structure, reproducible runs, observability, throughput awareness |
| Startup execution | Ship-and-iterate cadence; product taste; willingness to write public-facing prose |
| Infra thinking | You can talk about cost-per-token, latency, batching; pick the right model for the job |
| Rapid iteration | 21-day plan with daily commits is itself a portfolio piece |
| Unusual project taste | Phase-diagrams of failure, agent forensics, steering — not "I built a chatbot" |

## What your edge is NOT (don't compete here)

- Math olympiad / theory
- ML coursework / Kaggle leaderboard
- Big-lab pedigree
- Published research
- Years of PyTorch / CUDA muscle memory

When the application asks "why you?" — answer with your actual edge, not the missing ones.

## Why this works for MATS specifically

MATS mentors increasingly want **empirical, engineering-heavy contributors** who can build infrastructure, run experiments at scale, and write up results. They have plenty of people who can do theory. They are bottlenecked on people who can take a half-formed research question and turn it into:

1. A clean experiment harness
2. A dataset
3. Plots that tell the story
4. A 2000-word writeup that a busy reviewer can read in 8 minutes

Your background trains you to do exactly that. The 21 days are about proving it.

## Avoiding the "finance bro" trap

> *Your CV likely leans heavy on finance/quant. The CV is allowed to show that. The MATS project is not.*

The risk: reviewers pattern-match "ex-finance → wants to do AI for trading → not serious about alignment." Defuse it by:

- **Project domain is not finance.** Pick toy domains: science QA, code, multi-hop reasoning, agent tool-use. Never trading.
- **Motivation framing is safety-adjacent.** "I want to understand failure modes of deployed AI systems" — not "I want to use this in production."
- **Blog post avoids ROI / market language.** Use ML research vocabulary throughout.
- **Mentor selection skews safety-explicit.** See [[07-MATS-Application-Strategy]].

Your physics + systems background is a much stronger story than your finance background. Lead with the former.

## Confidence calibration

You will feel underqualified on Day 3. Everyone does. The MATS bar is **demonstrated traction in 3 weeks**, not "already a researcher." If you have:

- A working agent eval harness
- One genuinely interesting empirical finding
- A clear writeup
- A clean public repo

…you are in the top quartile of applicants. Most candidates submit a half-finished tutorial reimplementation and a vague essay. Don't be most candidates.
